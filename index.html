Corriente origin and roping-cattle market

The Belen, New Mexico weekly report now contributes unclassified Corriente price ranges. It does not identify whether cattle are native / U.S.-raised or Mexican M-branded, so those observations stay in the unclassified category. “Fresh Corrientes” are not automatically treated as Mexican M-branded.

The origin distinction matters in the roping-cattle trade, but public reporting describes contractor purchases and expectations rather than a consistent native-versus-M-branded auction series. The native and Mexican M-branded filters therefore only include records where the source or a manual entry explicitly identifies the origin. See The Team Roping Journal’s July 2026 market reporting and the Belen weekly auction reports.

Data coverage and interpretation

Loading published market observations…

State averages use state-specific auction or USDA state-summary records. Direct-trade, multi-state video, and regional summaries remain separate. Head-weighted averages use records with reported head counts; where head counts are unavailable, the state falls back to a simple observation average and is labeled accordingly. Corriente records without origin evidence are kept as unclassified; native and Mexican M-branded cattle are never inferred from the word “fresh” alone. Bred replacement heifers are shown separately from open / unbred replacement heifers. Idaho and Oregon feeder observations currently use public Cattle Channel sale summaries and may update at a different cadence; other state sources are a mix of USDA and local auction reports. Cattle Channel's state archive pages are not publicly crawlable, so new report links require periodic source discovery.

Decision framework

Buy feeder cattle

Compare local $/cwt against your calculated bid ceiling. Adjust for weight slide, shrink, freight, health, frame, flesh, and days required to reach 600 lb.

Buy replacement heifers

Evaluate genetics, disposition, structure, reproductive soundness, age, vaccination history, and expected lifetime calf output. Price alone is not a replacement strategy.

Sell spring calves

Track comparable steer and heifer lots separately. Record weight, sale type, breed composition, and market to build a reliable local benchmark.

Hold / market timing

Use seasonal patterns as context only. Confirm current bids and local supply; do not treat historical averages as guaranteed price forecasts.

Model assumptions

  • Revenue = hanging weight × price per hanging-weight pound.
  • Target margin = net profit ÷ gross revenue.
  • Maximum all-in cost = revenue × (1 − target margin).
  • Maximum purchase price subtracts feed, general, diesel allocation, vaccines, and other costs from allowable total costs.
  • Diesel is allocated by load size, not charged as a full load to each animal.
  • Processing is excluded because the current assumption is that the customer pays the processor separately.

Data integrity

This dashboard starts with no fabricated sale prices. Market history and seasonal signals will become useful only after comparable observations are entered.
  • Keep Visalia and Overland observations identifiable.
  • Do not mix Corriente, Angus-cross, and Holstein/dairy lots in one average.
  • Compare similar weight ranges and sale types.
  • Record lot averages rather than isolated high/low individual bids where possible.
  • Use enough observations across multiple years before interpreting seasonality.

Backup and data controls

Market observations are stored in this browser on this device. Export a CSV backup regularly. Importing a CSV appends rows; it does not replace existing data.
